Originality and the IISE catalog

The IE Hub is not affiliated with, endorsed by, sponsored by or licensed by the Institute of Industrial and Systems Engineers (IISE) or any other training provider.

The IISE Training Center publishes a catalog of professional courses. We used that public catalog in exactly one way: as a checklist of subjects an industrial-engineering curriculum should cover. Our category names and the course titles in the roadmap follow that catalog so that readers can see what is covered and what is still missing. A few courses also follow the order of topics listed in a public course outline, and say so in their README.

What we did not use: no IISE course materials, slides, handouts, workbooks, lesson text, exercises, exams, datasets, figures or instructor notes. None of the people who built these courses had access to that material while writing. Every lesson, worked example, dataset, figure, script and solution on this site was written independently (see the next section) and is original. Where a course covers a subject that has an official licensed data card or tables, such as MODAPTS or MTM-1, the course uses its own simplified teaching version and says so; the official data is not reproduced.

A list of topics is a statement of what a subject contains. We believe using it as a coverage map is fair and lawful; a reader learning time study, SPC or design of experiments needs the same list no matter who teaches it. If a rights holder disagrees about any specific page, the reporting process below applies and we will act on it promptly.

AI-assisted content and verification

The courses were written with substantial help from AI language models, guided by the contributors. That has two consequences you should know about:

  1. Mistakes are possible. Figures, formulas, standards references and historical claims can be wrong or out of date. Every dataset is simulated and every company and person named in a case is fictional.
  2. Each course carries a status. Draft means it has not yet been checked by a reviewer. Verified means a named reviewer has worked through the review checklist: originality (nothing copied from IISE or any other source), accuracy of the technical content, correct use of trademarks and licences, and that every number can be reproduced by the course's own scripts. The status is shown on every course card and at the top of every lesson.

Until a course is marked verified, treat it as a well-organised study guide to check against primary sources, not as a reference of record. The site as a whole is a preview release while that review is in progress.
